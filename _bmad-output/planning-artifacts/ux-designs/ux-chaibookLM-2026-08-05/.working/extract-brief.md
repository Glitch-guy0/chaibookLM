# Extract — brief + addendum (2026-08-04)

## Positioning & personality
- chaibookLM = "a personal research workspace in the spirit of Google's NotebookLM": upload sources, chat with an AI that answers strictly from your own materials, citing exactly where every claim came from.
- Stance vs NotebookLM: optimizes for *clarity, cost, and trust*; "simpler, cheaper, more honest"; wrapped in a bold, funky neo-brutalist identity deliberately unlike anything Google ships.
- Personality keywords: clarity, cost, trust; simpler, cheaper, more honest; funky, bold, modern, deliberately unique; calm, verifiable, inexpensive.
- Design pillar: "A unique, funky neo-brutalist + minimalistic-maximalist design language — bold, modern, unmistakably different from Google's clean minimalism." Honest read: "must be executed with taste."
- Differentiator table: Design/UX → genuine visible contrast (needs taste). Citation → original view → "**Genuine gap.** NotebookLM shows a highlighted quote preview; we show the source itself."
- Unfair advantage: no technical moat; advantage is execution and focus + citation experience that visibly closes the loop to the original document.

## Design identity anchors [ASSUMPTION]
- "Neo-brutalism + minimalistic maximalism — funky, modern, and deliberately unique; not premium/quiet." Brand-level contrast to NotebookLM's clean Google minimalism; lead differentiator on first impression.
- Practical anchors: thick hard borders, bold offset shadows, saturated/confident color palette, chunky playful typography, visible structure over airy whitespace — applied with discipline ("minimalistic maximalism: lots of personality, zero noise"). Web-first + mobile optimization: "the aesthetic must survive small screens."

## Personas & engagement
- Primary: everyday consumers learning deeply (studying, courses, mixed-source research, analyzing owned content). Success: "ask a question, get an answer they can verify against the original source in one click, without leaving the flow."
- Secondary: students/self-directed learners needing citation discipline (essays, reports, study notes); value a tool that doesn't require a Google account.
- Free tier must compete on experience, not quota alone.

## UX-relevant goals / success definitions
- Engagement: users create multiple notebooks; sources per notebook grow beyond first upload.
- Grounded answers: ≥90% of chat answers carry at least one citation to a user source.
- Citation trust: a meaningful share of citations clicked, click lands on the original view, not a fallback. (UX-critical loop.)
- Ingestion breadth: all source types ingested successfully in normal use.
- Uploads/indexing stay friction-free.

## Roadmap affecting UX architecture (beyond v0.1)
- v0.1: textarea + webpage, rich-metadata indexing, chat with per-source citations, citation → original view (renders the source).
- v0.3: PDF support — citations render the actual PDF page.
- v0.6: transcripts (.srt/.vtt) with timestamp-anchored indexing.
- v1: YouTube — timestamp is primary citation mark; clicking citation plays video at exact moment; design polish, mobile optimization, freemium credits.
- Credit UX (v1+, design-now): 10 credits/day, daily reset; each agent call (chat answer OR web search) consumes 1 credit; credits gate AI usage only — uploading, indexing, browsing sources remain free. Payments/billing out of scope.
- Vision (2-3 yr): audio sources, artifact generation, optional collaboration, self-hosted option.

## Look & feel / tone / first impressions
- Design identity = "lead differentiator on first impression," "instantly recognizable." Vision capstone: "The neo-brutalist design language becomes the product's signature."
- Three-surface shape UX must organize: Sources (upload/ingest), Chat (grounded Q&A with visible citations), Original view (citation destination — rendered page/PDF/video at exact timestamp).
- Citation-trust loop = emotional core: verify "in one click, without leaving the flow." Original view is the payoff moment.
- Tone: calm for research flow, confident/funky for brand; "cheaper, simpler, more honest."
