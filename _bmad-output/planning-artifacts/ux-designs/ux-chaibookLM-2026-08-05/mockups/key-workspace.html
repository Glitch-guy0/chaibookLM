What's the best way to avoid overwatering?

The most reliable way to avoid overwatering is to check the soil before you add water — press a finger about an inch into the pot, and water only when that layer feels dry.

Most houseplants prefer the soil to dry out a little between waterings, so a fixed weekly schedule often leaves roots wetter than they want to be.

Water thoroughly but rarely: pour until it runs from the drainage holes, then empty the saucer so the pot never sits in standing water.

Yellowing leaves and soft, mushy stems usually mean the roots are staying too wet — slow down and let the soil dry before the next watering.

How Plants Drink

Where the water goes

Plants pull water up from their roots and move it through the stem to the leaves, where most of it evaporates through tiny pores. This steady flow is what keeps a plant's cells firm and its leaves upright.

That same flow means watering isn't about adding more — it's about keeping the air pockets in the soil open, so roots can breathe and take up water exactly when the plant needs it.

Watering, simply

  • Water only when the top inch of soil is dry.
  • Always use a pot with drainage holes.
  • Empty the saucer after watering.
